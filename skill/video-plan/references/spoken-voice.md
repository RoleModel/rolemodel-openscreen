# Spoken Voice Layer

`/rolemodel-voice` governs the four voice concepts and the non-negotiables. This file adds what changes when the words are heard instead of read.

## What Carries Over Unchanged

The four concepts hold in speech, and two of them get stronger:

- **Humble Confidence.** Declarative, no hedging. Negation before assertion works even better aloud than on a page. "This is not a template. It is a practice."
- **Trusted Partnership.** Together, navigate, thought partners. Say "partner," never "client" or "customer."
- **Instructive Clarity.** Why before how. A metaphor spoken lands harder than a metaphor read, because the listener cannot skim past it.
- **Practical Value.** Every claim resolves to something concrete.

Non-negotiables hold: no em dashes, lead with why, "partner" not "client."

## What Changes For The Ear

### Contractions on, always

Written RoleModel voice tolerates "we have" and "it is." Spoken, they sound stilted. Use "we've," "it's," "you're," "that's," "here's." The only reason to expand a contraction is deliberate emphasis on a single word.

### Sentences under 18 words

Not a hard cap. A target. A listener has no way to re-read, so a sentence that needs holding in memory is a sentence that gets lost. When a sentence passes 18 words, look for the natural break and make it two.

Long is allowed once, for effect, when the length itself is the point.

### Fragments are fine

Written voice avoids them. Spoken voice needs them. "Every quote. By hand. Twice." is three fragments and it works, because that is how people talk when they mean it.

### Nothing that only works on a page

Cut these entirely from spoken script:

- **Parentheticals.** There is no audible parenthesis. Make it a sentence or cut it.
- **Semicolons.** If the script has one, the sentence is two sentences.
- **"e.g." and "i.e."** Say "like" or "which means."
- **Slashes.** "and/or" is unspeakable. Pick one.
- **Bulleted lists read aloud.** Three items spoken as a sentence, or three items on screen while the speaker says the point.
- **Footnote-style caveats.** A caveat spoken sounds like doubt. Either it matters enough for its own sentence or it does not belong.

### Numbers the way a person says them

| Written | Spoken |
|---|---|
| $1,250,000 | "one and a quarter million dollars" |
| 47.3% | "just under half" or "forty-seven percent" |
| 2026-08-26 | "late August" |
| 3.2x | "more than three times" |

Put the precise figure on screen. Say the version a person would say. Precision is the graphic's job, not the mouth's.

### Read it aloud

The single most useful check. Read every line out loud before committing it. If you stumble, the viewer will too, and the fix is a rewrite, not a comma.

Signs a line needs rewriting:
- You run out of breath before the period
- Two similar sounds collide
- You have to re-read to get the emphasis right
- The line only makes sense with the stress on one particular word

## Openings

The open defines the problem or the purpose. It does not greet and it does not tease.

Do not open with:
- "Hi, I'm ..." for anything under two minutes. Identity goes in the lower third.
- "In today's fast-moving ..." or any variant.
- "Have you ever ..." Rhetorical questions ask the viewer to do work before you have earned it.
- "I want to talk about ..." Talk about it.
- A tease that withholds. "What I'm about to show you will change how you quote" spends attention to promise attention.

Do open with:
- The problem, stated as the viewer experiences it. "Your team quotes every job by hand, and the estimate is stale before it ships."
- The purpose, stated plainly, for a demo or an update. "This is the revision history panel, and it takes two days out of your quoting loop."
- A specific fact that reframes. Only if it is genuinely surprising and genuinely relevant.

## Closings

Summarize in one or two lines, then one action. Then stop.

Do not close with:
- Multiple actions. Follow, subscribe, book, and share is zero actions.
- "Thanks for watching." Neutral at best.
- A new idea. The close is not where a fourth belief goes.
- A soft invitation. "Feel free to reach out if you'd like" is not an action.

Do close with:
- What they now believe, compressed. "Custom does not mean slow, and it does not mean fragile."
- One specific next step, with the friction named. "Book a Discover Phase call. It's ninety minutes and you leave with a prototype scope."

## Two Or More Speakers

- Each speaker sounds like themselves. Two speakers reading one voice is worse than one speaker.
- Never split a sentence across speakers. It reads as scripted, because it is.
- A handoff is a full stop. The new speaker starts a new thought.
- Do not write agreement. "Right, exactly" and "that's a great point" are the sound of a script pretending to be a conversation.
- Layer in `/personal-voice` if it is available in the session, since a named person on camera is personal-voice content. RoleModel voice wins on conflict.

## Captions

Assume captions, since most viewers watch with them on and many watch with sound off.

- Every word in the script gets captioned. Do not write a line that only works with vocal inflection.
- Burned-in captions and on-screen text compete for the same space. Note in the visual direction which beats have both, and keep on-screen text to seven words or fewer.
